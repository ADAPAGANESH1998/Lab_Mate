What Is DOI and Why Use It?

Definition: A DOI (Digital Object Identifier) is a permanent link to a publication.

Why Upload DOI Instead of PDFs?

DOIs provide a direct link to the journal-hosted version, ensuring legality. Users can still share their research without violating copyright.
Example: DOI: 10.1038/s41586-020-2649-2 (Leads to the Nature journal site).

Copyright & Publisher Policies

  • Understanding Rights
    • -Many journals own the copyright to your published work unless specified otherwise.
    • -You may only have permission to share the preprint (before peer review) or accepted manuscript (after peer review but before typesetting).
  • Types of Open Access Models:
    • 1.Gold Open Access – Authors pay an APC (Article Processing Charge) to make the article openly available.
    • 2.Green Open Access – Authors can self-archive their work after an embargo period.
    • 3.Hybrid Open Access – Combination of both models, often with optional fees for open access.
  • How LabMate Handles This?
    • -Users must ensure they have the legal right to upload full-text PDFs.
    • -LabMate will not be responsible for copyright violations.

Embargo Period

An embargo period is the time set by publishers during which a research article cannot be freely shared (usually after acceptance but before open-access availability).

  • ◆ Why It Matters?
    • -Publishers impose embargo periods to protect their subscription revenue.
    • -Uploading a full-text article during the embargo period may violate copyright agreements.
  • ◆ How to Check Embargo Period?
    • -Use the Sherpa/Romeo database to verify embargo policies of different publishers.
    • -Some journals allow self-archiving (Green Open Access) after a specified period.
    • Example:
    • -Elsevier: Typically, 12-24 months embargo.
    • -Springer: Varies, check journal-specific policies.
    • -Nature: Often does not allow self-archiving of published versions.

Self-Archiving

What It Is & Why It Matters

Definition

Self-archiving is the practice of authors uploading a version of their research paper to an open-access repository, institutional website, or preprint server, making it freely available to the public.

Why Is It Important?

  • Increases the visibility and accessibility of research.
  • Bypasses paywalls, allowing researchers worldwide to access scientific knowledge.
  • Complies with funding agency requirements for open-access publication.

Types of Self-Archived Versions

  • Preprint (Before Peer Review).
  • Post print (After Peer Review, Before Formatting)
  • Publisher’s PDF (Final Published Version)

Background colour code #00B0F0
Font:TimesnewRoman

➡➡

What Are Institutional Repositories?

Many universities allow researchers to upload their work to a trusted repository instead of third-party websites.
Example: arXiv, PubMed Central, Zenodo, ResearchGate.

What to Check Before Uploading?
Some journals permit self-archiving of the preprint version but not the final published version.
Always check journal policies before uploading.
